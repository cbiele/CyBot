# Axis 4 roll and forearm: compact at the back, aluminium drive tube

Date: 2026-11-15. Status: approved by the user, 2026-11-15.

## Intent

V2 keeps a small, light head (see the V2 direction notes). The V1 forearm and its Ø68 inline axis 4 holder are bulky. The user wants a much slimmer forearm out to the wrist, built around parts already in hand and the gearbox that has proved most repeatable: the 20:1 compact cycloid.

All the drive mass stays at the back of the forearm, near the elbow, so the elbow and shoulder lift less. The roll is carried out to the wrist by a slow-turning aluminium tube. The user does not want the gearbox at one end of the forearm and the motor or encoder at the other.

## Goals

- Axis 4 (roll) driven by a 20:1 compact cycloid at the elbow end of the forearm, with its motor beside it on a 188 GT2 belt (20T/40T, 40:1 total), the same belt as axes 5 and 6.
- The compact's output turns the user's 190 mm aluminium tube (9 mm ID × 11 mm OD) directly. That is what's left after 60 mm went to the T8 hollow. The tube turns at roll speed.
- A printed, fixed outer tube is the forearm structure. It carries the bending load. The aluminium tube spins inside it on bearings.
- The axis 5 fork (already on a 188 belt, compact on one arm) mounts on the front end of the aluminium tube, so axes 5, 6 and the hand roll with it.
- The 190 mm tube sets the forearm length. There is no target reach.

## Non-goals

- Wire routing. All wires run outside for now. Routing is solved last, only if the layout is kept.
- The elbow. That is the next design, also on a compact. This design only defines the elbow's attachment point: the back of the fixed outer tube, where the axis 4 compact's housing mounts.
- Changes to the compact cycloid itself, or to axis 5, axis 6 or the hand.
- The T8 hollow's tuning, which continues separately.

## Anti-goals

- Gearbox at the wrist end with the motor or encoder at the elbow end.
- A cheap planetary (the 4:1 geared NEMA17) on the roll: about 1–1.5× torque margin and 1–2° of backlash. It was considered and rejected.
- A forearm as bulky as V1's.

## Constraints

- Printers: Bambu A1 or P2S, bed 225 × 255 mm. The outer tube (about 183 mm) prints in one piece, standing on its flange.
- Aluminium tube: 9 × 11 mm, 190 mm long, in hand. It is the drive shaft, not the structure. Its twist is about 0.5° per N·m over 190 mm, which is acceptable.
- Worst-case roll load estimated at 0.77 N·m (from the head concept). The compact at 40:1 has well over 5× that unloaded.
- The compact's output face: front carrier hub, three M3 × 3 × 4.5 heat-set inserts on R6.5 at 90°, 210° and 330°. Proven unit, generated by `tools/blender/cycloid_poc.py` (default, no flags), housing Ø68.
- 188 motor mount: `cycloid_poc.py` builds it for the hollow sets (`MOTOR_X` from the 188 belt). The compact's mount is still the 232 version, so it needs a 188 variant.
- Printed press fits proven on this printer (see the print-fits notes): a printed Ø11.0 bore is a press fit on the tube (eccentric-tube-lobes, with CA glue). `tube-end-sleeve` (Ø12 × Ø11 × 4) fits a 6701 (12 × 18 × 4) and needs Arachne in the slicer. Layer height is 0.12.
- The user prints parts and fit-tests them on real hardware rather than on coupons.

## Decided by the user

- A compact at the back drives the tube (over the 4:1 gearmotor, and over a cycloid at the wrist end).
- 188 belt on axis 4.
- Wires stay external for now.
- The 190 mm tube decides the forearm length.
- The elbow comes next, on a 20:1 compact. It sits behind and below the axis 4 compact, as on V1, so the axis 4 motor goes on the top or sides of the housing, clear of that space.
- No printed axis 5 fork exists yet. This design ends in a mounting flange on the front of the tube. The future fork adopts the flange's pattern.

## My calls (the user has not reviewed these)

- **Compact unchanged:** the tube is driven through a bolt-on output plate on the existing three M3 inserts. The proven carrier is not reprinted. The screw heads on R6.5 overlap an Ø11 socket, so the plate drives a separate tube hub through three dogs. The hub has the Ø11 press-fit socket, about 15 mm deep, plus CA glue.
- **Encoder:** sits with the compact's output at the back. Its mounting is out of scope until an encoder is chosen.
- **Tube bearings:** 6701s on printed Ø12 sleeves: one just in front of the output adapter and one at the front end of the outer tube. The bearings sit in seats in the outer tube.
- **Outer tube:** about Ø25–30 mm, printed. It bolts to the compact's housing at the back (the housing's four M3 bolt holes on R30 are a candidate) and carries the front 6701 seat at the wrist end.
- **Fork flange:** a printed hub with an Ø11 press-fit socket (plus CA glue, or a clamp) on the front of the tube. It ends in a flange of about Ø30 with four M3 heat-set inserts, sized for the head's bending moment and the roll torque. The builder sets the exact diameter and pattern, and both are recorded for the fork design.
- **Motor:** a pancake, like axes 5 and 6, at 40:1 (about 1.7 N·m usable against the 0.77 N·m roll load). This replaces the 34 mm motor in the inline concept.
- **Placement in the concept model:** V1's elbow axis and roll height (z 555) stay fixed, and the wrist pitch position follows from the tube. The V1 holder stays in the model as a ghost for comparison but drops out of the clash checks.
- **Order:** generate the printable parts first, then lay them out in the head concept model (`tools/blender/head_concept.py`, new `--tube-forearm` variant), so the layout shows the real parts. Nothing is printed until the user has seen the layout.
- **Axis 4 motor mount:** the existing 188 mount `model/cycloid-poc/20-1-hollow/motor-mount.stl` fits the compact (same Ø68 housing, R30 bolts, Ø24 hub clearance). Its three extra post holes go unused. The axis 4 motor goes on top, above the roll axis, clear of the elbow below.

## Left to the builder

- Exact outer-tube diameter, wall and bearing-seat details.
- How the adapter and hub grip the tube: a press fit and glue, or a clamp.
- How the outer tube splits for printing, if it is too long for the printer.
- The motor's position around the compact, which must keep clear of the outer tube.
- Checks in the generator script, following the existing scripts' style (non-manifold, bore diameters, clearances).
